3. Aggressive Cows
Scenario: Farmer John has built a new long barn, with N stalls. The stalls are located along a 
straight line at positions x1, x2, ..., xN.

His C (cows) are aggressive towards each other. To prevent the cows from hurting each other, 
he wants to assign the cows to the stalls such that the minimum distance between any two of them is as large as possible.

Input:

stalls: An array of integers representing the coordinates of the stalls (e.g., [1, 2, 8, 4, 9]).

k: The number of cows you need to place (e.g., 3).

Output:

The largest possible value for the minimum distance.




4. "Magnetic Force Between Two Balls"
(LeetCode 1552 / Classic "Aggressive Cows")

Scenario: You have m magnetic balls and several baskets located at specific positions on a straight line.

Input:
position: An array of integers representing the location of each basket (e.g., [1, 2, 3, 4, 7]).
m: The number of balls you need to place.

Rules:
You must place all m balls into the baskets (one ball per basket).
The "Magnetic Force" is the distance between two balls.
If you place balls too close, the force is weak (bad). You want them as far apart as possible.

Goal: Distribute the balls such that the minimum distance between any two balls is maximized.

Example Case
Positions: [1, 2, 3, 4, 7]
Balls (m): 3




5. "Koko eating bananas"

Koko loves to eat bananas. There are n piles of bananas, ith pile has piles[i] bananas.
The guards have gone and will come back in h hours. Koko can decide her bananas-per-hour eating speed of k.
Each hour, she chooses some pile of bananas and eats k bananas from that pile.
If the pile has less than k bananas, she eats all of them instead and will not eat any more bananas during this hour.

Goal: Return the minimum integer k such that she can eat all the bananas within h hours.

Example Case
Piles: [3, 6, 7, 11]

Hours (h): 8
